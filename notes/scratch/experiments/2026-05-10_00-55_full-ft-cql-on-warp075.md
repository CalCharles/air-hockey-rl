## Question

The residual-RL canonical recipe on `warp075_p30` is **CQL α=20 + actor_updates=2 + N=5** (1M back-half mean 97, peak 170 — see [2026-05-08_02-17_cql-campaign.md](2026-05-08_02-17_cql-campaign.md)). Two questions:

1. Does the residual recipe transfer to **full-network** fine-tune (no frozen base, no residual head)?
2. Independently, what does plain full-FT look like at 500k on the new canonical target? Prior 100k full-FT baselines on the deprecated `sim2sim_combined` target peaked at 108 then drifted; never tested at 500k on `warp075_p30`.

**Shape on canonical (warp075_p30)**: monotonic rise from start (smoothed ~30 at step 5k) for the first 100–200k steps, smoothed peak in the 100–160k window, then a slow decay back toward 60–80 by 500k. seed 0 peaks higher and decays more gently than seed 1 — back-half means differ by 28 (86 vs 58), so cross-seed variance is large for this method. This is the same "FT past peak" drift documented in the 2026-04-25 full-FT campaign on the deprecated combined target.

**Shape on cross-env (env_mild_p10)**: same rise (96→125 by 200k), but the back-half decay is much milder — smoothed value sits at 115–125 across 200–500k. Easier env → more headroom → less destructive drift, exactly the pattern residual showed too.

### CQL variants (B, C, D — all combinations): flat from step ~10k

All eight CQL cells (4 variants × 2 seeds, plus 3 cross-env replicas) show the same trajectory:

- Smoothed value at step 50k: 17–19
- Smoothed value at step 500k: 17–19
- All inter-window slopes: **flat** (|slope| < 0.5 / 100k)
- Smoothed peak step is either the leading edge of the window or noise-driven (e.g., `peak @ 433k` for C_cql20_seed0 with peak value 18.6 vs floor 17 — 1.6-unit "peak" is within smoothed σ)
- Cross-seed reproducibility is excellent (e.g., B_cql20: seed0 back-half 17.26, seed1 18.37 — Δ ≈ 1)

The cells never rise above ~20 smoothed. Combined with the A_baseline data, this is **not a hyperparameter problem** — it's that the CQL penalty at α=20 is incompatible with full-FT initialization from a strong source policy at this LR scale. Tightening (D, full LR) didn't help; varying the architecture (C, N=5 + actor=2) didn't help; even on the easier env_mild_p10 target the cells stay flat.

The training-curve evidence alone is enough to conclude: **CQL-augmented recipes that work for residual fine-tune do not transfer to full-network fine-tune on this task.**

### C_cql20_actor2_n5 deterministic trajectory

Both seeds: **every checkpoint evaluates to mean=17.32, std=12.73, tail10=16.90, max=45**, with per-episode return distributions bit-identical across all 49 ckpts on seed0 and 29 ckpts on seed1.

This is a stronger claim than "the policy doesn't improve":

1. Bit-identical per-episode returns across checkpoints means the deterministic action sequence is identical for the same env init across all checkpoints. The actor function `s → a` produces the same `a` at every step of every eval episode regardless of which checkpoint's weights are loaded.
2. Bit-identical across **both seeds** means seed 0 and seed 1 — different random init, different replay sampling — converged to the same effective policy.
3. The most likely mechanism: the actor's tanh outputs are saturated (output ≈ ±1 on both action dims) for all observations in the eval distribution. Once saturated, `tanh'(±5+)` ≈ 0, so gradients can't pull the network back. CQL with α=20 dominates the actor-loss task term, and within the first ~10k steps the network finds and locks into a saturated fixed point. The replay then fills with ~17-return trajectories, the critic fits that distribution, and there's no signal to escape.

We did not directly verify saturation by inspecting actor outputs, but the bit-identical eval evidence is consistent with that hypothesis and inconsistent with "small-but-bounded action drift across checkpoints."

## Conclusion

**Headline**: full-network fine-tuning on `warp075_p30` is the inverse story of residual fine-tune. CQL is the load-bearing knob in residual; **CQL is fatal in full-FT**. Plain low-LR full-FT works, peaks early, and slowly drifts.

### What we know with high confidence

1. **CQL α=20 is incompatible with full-FT in this regime**, regardless of whether residual's other knobs are present (actor_updates=2, N=5) and regardless of whether LRs are restored (residual's full LR or FT's lr÷10). Same flat trajectory for B/C/D × seed0/seed1 × p30/p10.
2. **Plain full-FT (A_baseline, lr÷10) reaches a competitive peak**: smoothed training peak 91–106 on canonical (at ~100–160k), 129 on env_mild_p10 (sustained 115–125 across 200–500k). For comparison: residual canonical recipe back-half mean 97 / peak 170 at 1M on the same canonical target; env_mild_p10 mean 117 / peak 177 at 1M. Plain full-FT at 500k is in striking distance of residual at 1M, especially on env_mild_p10.
3. **Plain full-FT drifts past peak on the canonical target.** This was already documented for the 100k campaign on the old combined target; confirmed again here over 500k. Drift is much milder on the easier env_mild_p10 target.
4. **Cross-seed variance for plain full-FT is high.** A_baseline seeds 0 and 1 have back-half means of 86 vs 58 — Δ≈28. Need ≥3 seeds before claiming a definitive head-to-head with residual.

### Mechanism hypothesis

In residual mode the source actor is frozen and the residual head is `±0.15`-bounded; CQL's effect on Q-values can only nudge the residual head, which starts at zero. The base policy's good behavior keeps the replay buffer rich with high-return trajectories, and the residual head learns small corrections.

In full-FT mode the actor IS the policy. CQL pushes Q down on whatever the actor outputs vs `cql_n_random=10` uniform actions in `[−1, 1]^2`. With α=20 dominating the actor-loss task term, the gradient pulls the actor away from its current output toward... apparently a degenerate fixed point. Once there, the deterministic actor's output is constant enough that ckpt-to-ckpt eval is bit-identical. The replay buffer fills with degenerate trajectories (return ~17), the critic fits that distribution, and there's no signal pulling the actor back toward the source manifold.

### What is untested

- α < 5. The residual hyperparam sweep found α sweet zone 5–20 for residual; lower α might let full-FT escape the fixed point. Cheap experiment: 1 cell with α=2 or α=5 at 500k on canonical.
- BC anchor (`residual_action_l2` as TD3+BC since base = source). The residual sweep found BC stacking hurts in residual (`λ=0.5 → −10`), but in full-FT it might be the right safety net since the actor lacks a frozen base. Cheap: 1 cell with `residual_action_l2 = 0.5` (also requires turning on residual mode? — check; the L2 penalty is on the residual action, may not apply in fine_tune mode).
- 3rd seed on A_baseline. Current Δ between seeds is 28 on back-half mean — too noisy to claim plain full-FT vs residual head-to-head.
- Longer FT budget (1M). A_baseline already drifted past peak by 500k, so longer is unlikely to help on canonical. On env_mild_p10 the curve was still ~115 at 500k with no clear decay — 1M might either plateau or slowly improve.

### Recommendations

- **Don't update the canonical recipe doc or CLAUDE.md yet.** Residual-RL recipe with CQL still wins by peak/mean at 1M; full-FT shows it's a viable simpler alternative on env_mild_p10 but lower confidence. Need 3rd seed + 1M extension on the best A cell before a head-to-head claim.
- **Next experiment** (small): A_baseline seed 2 at 500k on canonical + env_mild_p10. Resolve the cross-seed variance question. If A_baseline seed 2's back-half mean is closer to seed 0 (86) than seed 1 (58), plain full-FT becomes a real challenger.
- **Optional**: 1 cell with α=2 to test whether CQL is salvageable at all in full-FT.
