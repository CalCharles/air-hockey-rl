## Question
The best new-sim TD3 recipe (`configs/ceiling/scratch_newsim.yaml`, `rma_mode: history_privileged`) works largely because
of its inputs: the actor reads a 50-step window through a conv adaptation module, and the critic reads the flat window plus
the simulator state. Do the same inputs help CRL (paper and c0) learn stop goals (x, y, 0, 0)?

## Conclusions
- **No gain.** Every E1 metric is within about 1 std err of its baseline except the paired gap on set B, which is lower for
  both algorithms (paper −0.003 vs 0.051, c0 0.004 vs 0.023): the policies use the goal no more, arguably less.
  Stop-on-target on set B is slightly higher (7 vs 4 %, 3 vs 2.2 %) but inside the single-seed noise and swings 1–11 %
  across checkpoints with no upward trend.
- Training episodes got longer for paper (~300 steps at 5M vs ~130 in eval), so the richer inputs may help keeping the puck
  in play, but this does not show up as goal reaching.
- The bottleneck is not the critic's view of the state. It is consistent with the explanation in the earlier notes: stopped
  puck states are rare in the data, so ψ((x, y, 0, 0)) is extrapolated and the actor gets a weak signal. The 4-D-goal c0
  (12.4 %) remains the best stop-goal policy.
- 1 seed per algorithm: only large effects would be trustworthy, and none appeared. E2–E4 (actor-privileged, conv critic,
  no critic window) were not run.
